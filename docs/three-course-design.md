# Whiteout, Monsoon and Cinderline

Three original courses extend the six-course roster. Each uses a separate authored plan/elevation, a grounded landscape and a full-sphere panorama. The existing three layouts and record keys are retained; the additions use `whiteout-1`, `monsoon-1` and `cinderline-1`.

## Whiteout — polar observatory

A high research plateau feeds a broad, roughly 270° descending spiral inside a blue glacial basin. A separate valley climbs back through weather stations and a return fork. Dark asphalt and blue/white rails preserve the racing line against ice; grounded orange instruments give scale. The horizon separates sharp snowy peaks, a low research settlement and open polar sea.

Weapons prepare the basin entry; the accessible boost follows its exit. The return fork trades an inner acceleration line against outer armament. No boost is placed inside the long descending spiral.

## Monsoon — hydraulic city

Terraced green highlands descend into a curving dry lock, below adjacent retained reservoirs. The open sky, tall water-retaining walls and pump island distinguish it from an enclosed tunnel. A shorter technical service fork trades against a faster outer spillway line. Water stays outside the racing envelope.

Armament sits before the descent and inside the dry chamber. Boosts reward the chamber exit, outer fork and uphill recovery, not the blind descent. Cool green light, ceramic housing terraces and cloud-heavy wet highland silhouettes establish the atmosphere.

## Cinderline — volcanic upland

Three broad continuous-deck waves alternate compressions, climbs and rounded crests. Direction changes sit in valleys; the route has no artificial gap. A banked caldera bend and a basalt-ridge return connect geothermal works, sulfur shoulders and cool-white turbine halls. The panorama has an asymmetric volcanic ridge and a drifting ash plume, not a red desert palette.

An accessible boost follows the first compression; a second follows the final wave. Weapons sit on readable setup and valley lines rather than blind crests.
